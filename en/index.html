About

How I work

Whatever the website is about, the goal is the same: the right person finds the right page and takes the next step. So I care less about traffic for its own sake and more about whether those visits turn into calls, sales or inquiries.

I start by finding what’s really holding a site back: technical problems, weak pages or missing keywords. Then I fix it, write what’s missing, and send you a report with numbers from Search Console and GA4, so you can see for yourself what changed.

I work a lot in Arabic, which matters more than people think. People type the same question in different dialects and spellings, and a page that matches only one of them loses visitors. I take that into account when I plan keywords and write pages.

I’m also honest about what SEO can and can’t do. It isn’t instant, nobody can guarantee a number one spot, and a good plan beats a quick trick every time. If your site needs something other than SEO first, I’ll tell you.

If you’ve been burned by an SEO before, I understand the hesitation. That’s why I explain every step in plain language, show you the numbers each month, and never ask you to take my word for it.

I also like you to understand what’s happening on your own site. So I explain the numbers simply, say why we chose one step before another, and leave the final decision to you.

Signs

Signs your website needs SEO

If you recognise yourself in any of these, there’s probably something worth fixing.

  • You show up when people search your name, but for nothing else.
  • Competitors who are no better than you appear above you on Google.
  • You publish content regularly and it brings almost no visitors.
  • The site is slow, or looks fine but Google isn’t indexing its pages properly.
  • Visitors arrive and leave without calling, buying or filling in a form.
  • You redesigned or moved the website and the traffic dropped.

Process

What working together looks like

No mystery and no jargon. Here’s how a typical project goes.

I review your site

I look at your website the way Google does. Can it be crawled and indexed? Is it fast? Is it organised in a way that makes sense? I also check which pages already bring visits and which ones nobody sees.

I find the opportunities

Next I research what your customers type into Google and what your competitors already rank for. The result is a list of searches worth going after, each matched to a page that should answer it.

We agree on a plan

I turn everything into a short, prioritised plan: what to fix first, what to write, what can wait. You see it before anything changes, and you decide what goes ahead.

I fix and write

Then I get to work: technical fixes, page improvements, new content and internal links. If a developer is involved, I send clear instructions so nothing gets lost along the way.

I measure and adjust

Every month you get a plain report from Search Console and GA4: what moved, what didn’t, and what I’m changing next. SEO is never really finished, so we keep improving from the data.

What to expect, honestly

SEO is a slow burn. Small technical fixes can show up within weeks, but most websites need three to six months before the changes add up to a clear difference in clicks and enquiries. Competitive searches take longer. Anyone who promises the first position in a week is selling you something.

What you can expect from me is steady progress you can see in the numbers, clear explanations in plain language, and a straight answer when something isn’t working.

Common mistakes

Mistakes I fix again and again

Most websites share a handful of problems. These are the ones I see most.

Targeting words nobody searches

Pages are often built around the words a business uses internally, not the words customers type. Good research fixes that before a single page gets written.

One page for everything

Putting every service on a single page makes life hard for Google and for visitors. Each service or topic deserves its own page that answers one question well.

Ignoring speed on mobile

Most visits come from phones. A site that takes five seconds to load loses people before they read a word, and Google notices that too.

Thin or copied content

Short pages that repeat what everyone else says rarely rank. Useful, specific content written for your actual customers does.

Broken internal links

Pages that nothing links to are hard for Google to find, and visitors get lost. A clean linking structure helps both.

No tracking

Plenty of sites run for years without Search Console or Analytics set up properly, so nobody knows what’s working. That’s usually the first thing I fix.

Duplicate pages

The same content on several addresses splits its strength between them. Redirects and canonical tags put it back in one place.

Forgetting the next step

A page can rank well and still earn nothing if there’s no clear button, phone number or form. Ranking brings the visit, the page has to finish the job.

FAQ

Questions people ask me

How long does it take to see results?

Usually three to six months for a clear difference, sometimes sooner for technical fixes. It depends on how competitive your searches are and how much needs fixing. I’ll give you a realistic idea after the first review.

Can you guarantee the first spot on Google?

No, and be careful with anyone who does. Google decides the rankings, not me. What I can promise is a clear plan, careful work and honest reporting, and the numbers show whether it’s working.

What kind of websites do you work on?

Company sites, blogs, online stores, service websites, directories and more. The details change from one site to another, but the basics are the same: help Google understand the site and help visitors find what they came for.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the size of the website and how much work it needs, so I don’t quote a fixed price before seeing it. Send me the link, tell me your goal, and I’ll come back with a clear proposal.

What do you need from me to start?

Access to Google Search Console and Analytics if you have them, a quick chat about your business and customers, and the competitors you keep an eye on. If none of that is set up yet, I can help you set it up.

Will you change my website without asking?

No. I show you what I plan to change and why before I touch anything. You approve, then I either make the changes or send clear instructions to your developer.

How will I know it’s working?

You get a monthly report with clicks, impressions, rankings and visits, explained in plain words. The goal is always more calls, sales or enquiries, so we look at those too, not just rankings.

Do you work on Arabic websites?

Yes, a large part of my work is on Arabic websites, and I also work in English. I can handle bilingual sites too, including the language setup Google needs to show the right version to the right person.

What are AI Overviews, and do they matter?

They are the AI summaries Google now shows above some results. Pages that answer questions clearly and are well structured are more likely to be used in them, so I write and organise content with that in mind.

Do I need ongoing SEO or a one-time job?

Some sites only need a one-off clean-up and a plan. Most do better with ongoing work, because search keeps changing and competitors keep publishing. We can start small and decide after the first months.

Do you build or redesign websites?

My focus is search. I work on your existing site, in WordPress or whatever platform you use, or alongside your developer. If a redesign is planned, I’m happy to advise on the structure so you don’t lose the traffic you already have.

What tools do you use?

Search Console and GA4 for the facts, Semrush, Ahrefs and Screaming Frog for research and audits, and WordPress or whatever platform your site runs on for the changes.

Can you help if my traffic suddenly dropped?

Yes. I check for technical issues, algorithm updates, lost links and pages that changed, find out what moved, and put together a recovery plan. Sudden drops usually have a cause that can be found.

Do you work with agencies or only with business owners?

Both. I can handle the SEO side for an agency’s clients quietly in the background, or work directly with the business owner. Whatever suits you better.

How often will we talk?

As often as you like, but I keep it light: a short call at the start, a monthly report with a summary, and a message whenever something important comes up.
